Day 002

Bayesian Statistics

Statistics

Kindergarten Explanation

Remember flipping that coin? Probability told you it's 50-50 for heads or tails. But what if I told you this coin is special—it's been landing on heads A LOT lately? Should you still bet 50-50? This is where Bayesian statistics comes in: it's about updating your guesses when you get new information.

Imagine you're a detective. You think there's a 30% chance it'll rain today (your starting guess). Then you look outside and see dark clouds. Aha! New clue! Now you update your guess to 70% chance of rain. That's Bayesian thinking: start with what you believe, then adjust as you learn more.

Here's the magic: you don't throw away your old guess—you blend it with the new evidence. If you've lived in your town for years and it rarely rains, those clouds might bump your guess from 30% to 50%. But if you just moved from the desert and don't know the weather yet, those same clouds might make you jump to 90%. Your prior knowledge matters. Bayesian stats is like being a scientist who gets smarter with every experiment, constantly refining predictions instead of starting from zero each time.

Why It Matters

Bayesian thinking powers everything from spam filters (updating the probability an email is junk) to medical diagnoses (revising the chance you have a disease based on test results).

Quick Exercise

You think there's a 20% chance your friend will call tonight. Then you remember it's their birthday. Does this new info increase or decrease the probability? By how much? Make your best guess!
