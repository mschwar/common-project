# Power Laws

## Kindergarten Explanation

Look around your neighborhood. Most houses are normal-sized, but there's probably one mansion that's WAY bigger than the rest. Look at your bookshelf: most books are thin, but one encyclopedia is super thick. This pattern—where a few things are HUGE and most are small—is called a power law.

Here's the weird part: it's not random. In a normal pattern (like people's heights), most are in the middle and extremes are rare. But power laws are different: there's no "typical" size. Most events are tiny, a few are medium, and a very few are absolutely enormous. Think earthquakes: thousands of little tremors, a handful of big shakes, and one catastrophic quake every few decades.

Why does this happen? Imagine a snowball rolling downhill. Small snowballs stay small. But if one gets a head start—maybe it rolls over extra snow—it grows bigger, which helps it pick up even MORE snow, which makes it even bigger. Success breeds success. The rich get richer, popular videos get more views, and big earthquakes release more energy. This "rich-get-richer" feedback loop creates power laws everywhere: wealth, city sizes, word frequencies, even website traffic.

## Why It Matters

Power laws explain why a few events dominate: 80% of wealth held by 20% of people, most book sales from a few bestsellers, most influence from a handful of social media posts.
